Secure Data Erasure

Deleting and formatting do not remove data — recovering it afterwards is literally our day job. When a device leaves your hands, sanitise it properly and get a certificate proving it.

Security service

How we sanitise media

Different media need different methods. Applying the wrong one quietly leaves data behind.

NIST 800-88 Clear

Full-media overwrite for hard drives, verified afterwards — appropriate for devices staying inside your organisation.

NIST 800-88 Purge

Stronger sanitisation for devices leaving your control, using vendor sanitise commands and block erase where supported.

Crypto-Erase for SSDs

On self-encrypting drives, destroying the key renders every block unreadable in seconds — the correct method for modern SSDs, where overwriting is unreliable.

Flash-Aware Handling

SSDs keep spare blocks invisible to overwrite tools. We use controller-level sanitisation rather than pretending a hard-drive method works.

Physical Destruction

For failed drives that no longer respond to any command, shredding or degaussing with documented destruction records.

Erasure Certificates

A per-device certificate with serial number, method and verification result — the paperwork auditors and clients ask for.

When you need it

Common triggers

Any time storage changes hands, sanitisation should happen first.

Selling or Returning Devices

Trade-ins, leased equipment and resold laptops routinely reach new owners with recoverable data still on them.

Employee Offboarding

Reassigned laptops and phones sanitised before the next person receives them.

Retirement & Compliance

End-of-life disposal with the documented evidence trail that data-protection obligations expect.

Retiring devices? Sanitise them first.

We will sanitise to the right standard for each medium and issue certificates for your records.
