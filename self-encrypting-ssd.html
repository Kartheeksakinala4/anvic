Self-Encrypting SSDs

Internal drives that encrypt everything they write, at full speed, with no performance cost — and can be sanitised in seconds at end of life. Most people who own one never switch it on.

Security product

How SED and OPAL work

The encryption engine sits inside the drive controller. Every block is encrypted whether you configure it or not — activation is what makes it protect you.

Transparent Encryption

Data encrypted as it is written by dedicated hardware. No CPU cost, no measurable performance impact, nothing for users to remember.

TCG OPAL Management

Standards-based activation and unlock, manageable through your operating system or management software rather than a vendor silo.

Pre-Boot Authentication

The drive stays locked until authenticated at power-on, so an SSD pulled from a stolen laptop is unreadable elsewhere.

Instant Crypto-Erase

Sanitised in seconds by destroying the encryption key — no overwrite pass, and reliable on flash where overwriting is not.

Correctly Activated

The step almost everyone skips: an unactivated SED protects nothing. We enable it, verify it, and document the credentials.

Recovery Planning First

We set up key escrow before activation — an activated SED with a lost key is data no one can recover, including us.

Honest guidance

What to know before you buy

Three things we tell every customer considering SEDs.

Activation Is Everything

Shipping with an encryption engine is not the same as being encrypted. Unactivated, the drive hands its data to anyone who plugs it in.

Recovery Becomes Absolute

Once activated, a lost key means unrecoverable data — even for a lab. That is the point, and exactly why escrow comes first.

Not a Backup

Encryption protects confidentiality, not availability. Pair it with a backup strategy that survives drive failure.

Want encryption you cannot forget to switch on?
